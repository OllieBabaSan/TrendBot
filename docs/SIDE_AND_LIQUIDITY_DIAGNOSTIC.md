# Side and liquidity diagnostic

Date: 2026-10-12

## Question

Does the funded 50% fast-transition candidate fail because shorts are broadly
defective, because funding destroys the long edge, or because only a subset of
short markets is executable enough for the trend signal to survive?

The repeatable study is `scripts\run_hyperliquid_side_diagnostics.py`. Complete
results are in `outputs\hyperliquid-side-diagnostics.json` and
`outputs\binance-side-diagnostics.json`.

## Diagnostic bounds

On funded Hyperliquid at 10 bps:

| Mode | Return | Sharpe | Drawdown | 2023 | 2024-25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Frozen 50% long/short | +26.10% | 0.318 | -30.43% | +33.52% | -12.64% |
| Long-only diagnostic | +38.84% | 0.455 | -19.95% | +8.35% | +6.56% |
| Short-only diagnostic | +0.23% | 0.072 | -24.83% | +16.44% | +2.44% |

Long-only improves Hyperliquid, but the broader archive rejects a simple
long-only promotion. On 638 historical Binance markets it returns +110.91%
with 0.736 Sharpe and -36.36% drawdown, versus +182.39%, 0.926, and -25.68%
for the frozen long/short candidate. Long-only loses 31.6% in 2022 and 7.3% in
2025. Removing shorts exchanges one transition problem for a less stable,
lower-quality return path.

Short-only is not viable: it is approximately flat on Hyperliquid and loses
26.17% on the broad archive, with -0.176 Sharpe and -42.57% drawdown.

## Causal partition findings

The audit used only information available before execution: listing age, prior
30-day median quote volume, lagged BTC 64/192 trend sign, and prior seven-day
absolute funding burden.

- BTC slow bull versus bear does not isolate the failure. Hyperliquid shorts
  lose in both regimes; broad-archive shorts are mildly positive in both.
- Low expected funding burden is not protective. It contains the worst
  Hyperliquid short contribution, so a funding threshold is not the answer.
- Listing age is inconsistent across venues except that the 366-730-day bucket
  is positive in both. Selecting that narrow age band now would be post-hoc.
- The highest prior-liquidity third is the only economically plausible short
  partition with positive contribution on both venues. Hyperliquid's middle
  third is strongly negative; the broad archive's low third is weakly negative.

## Exploratory hybrid

Because liquidity is causal, directly related to execution quality, and agrees
across venues, one deliberately post-hoc hypothesis was measured: retain every
long generated by the frozen 50% valve, but permit shorts only when a market is
in the top third of prior 30-day median quote volume among currently tradeable
markets. The two-thirds boundary came from the inspected attribution buckets;
therefore this is **not an untouched validation**.

At 10 bps:

| Dataset | Return | Sharpe | Drawdown | 2023 | 2024-25 | Net without top 5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Funded Hyperliquid | +42.57% | 0.499 | -25.31% | +19.79% | +3.34% | +7.74 pts |
| 638-market archive | +185.81% | 1.092 | -20.08% | +10.96% | +4.69% | +75.56 pts |

The ordering survives 20 and 30 bps. At 30 bps it returns +33.81% / 0.422 on
Hyperliquid and +161.50% / 1.007 on the broad archive. However, broad-archive
2022 is slightly negative and 2025 loses roughly 8-10% depending on costs.
Hyperliquid 2024-2025 is only marginally positive and becomes approximately
flat at 30 bps.

## Conclusion

This is the strongest coherent refinement uncovered by the investigation. It
improves risk-adjusted results, drawdown, transition-year behavior, cross-venue
direction, cost tolerance, and outlier dependence simultaneously. It also has
an economic mechanism: less-liquid perpetual shorts are more exposed to
slippage, squeezes, discontinuous listings, and unreliable price discovery.

It is still not a working or validated model. The rule was discovered from
inspected results, Hyperliquid Sharpe remains only 0.499, and its recent-period
margin is thin. Freeze it as a new research hypothesis (not a replacement for
T1/50%), apply venue-executable sizing and slippage, and seek genuinely new
forward evidence. Do not optimize the liquidity percentile on these samples.
